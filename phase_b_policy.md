# Synthetic Voice and Media Policy

**Maplewood Unified School District**
Communications, Technology, and Family Engagement
Effective: October 2026 | Review: annually, or within 30 days of any incident
under Section 8

---

## Who this is for and why it exists

This policy applies to every employee, contractor, vendor, and volunteer who
produces communications on behalf of Maplewood Unified. That includes the
Communications Office, school principals and their administrative assistants,
the Technology Department, Family Engagement staff, athletics and activities
coordinators, and any outside vendor producing audio or video under district
contract.

**What we are.** Maplewood Unified serves roughly 9,400 students across eleven
schools. We employ about 1,100 people. We communicate with families constantly:
automated attendance calls, weather and closure notifications, emergency
notifications, enrollment and registration reminders, nurse and health notices,
translated material for the roughly 22% of our families whose primary language is
not English, and ordinary school-level newsletters and announcements.

**Why this is a live question and not a theoretical one.** We already send
recorded voice calls to every household in the district. The infrastructure
exists, families are accustomed to it, and nobody questions a call that opens
with a familiar administrator's voice. That is exactly what makes synthetic voice
tempting here — it would let us produce those calls faster, in more languages,
without pulling a principal out of a classroom to record. It is also exactly what
makes it dangerous. Our families have been trained by us to trust an automated
call from a district number. That trust is an asset we built and it is an asset
we can destroy.

**The specific thing we are protecting.** When this district calls a family to
say there is an emergency at their child's school, that family must believe us
immediately and without hesitation. Everything in this policy is subordinate to
protecting that one moment.

---

## 1. Permitted uses

The following uses are permitted, subject to the disclosure requirements in
Section 4, the provenance requirements in Section 5, and the review requirements
in Section 6.

**1.1 Translation and language access.** Synthetic voice may be used to produce
translated versions of routine district communications for families whose
primary language is not English, provided the English original was written and
approved by a district employee and the translation has been reviewed by a
qualified human translator before synthesis. This is the use case with the
clearest benefit to families and the one this policy most wants to enable.

**1.2 Accessibility.** Synthetic voice may be used to produce audio versions of
written district material for families or students with visual impairments or
reading disabilities, and to produce written transcripts of recorded audio.

**1.3 Routine administrative notifications.** Synthetic voice may be used for
scheduled, non-urgent, non-emergency notifications where the content is factual
and administrative: enrollment windows, picture day, report card distribution,
bus route changes announced more than 24 hours in advance, fee deadlines, and
similar.

**1.4 Instructional and training material.** Synthetic voice or video may be used
in staff training, professional development content, and instructional material
produced by the district, provided no real person's voice or likeness is
replicated and the material is disclosed under Section 4.

**1.5 Draft and internal production work.** Synthetic voice may be used to
prototype a message internally — to hear how a script sounds, to time a
recording, to test a phone tree — provided the output is not distributed outside
the district and is deleted when the production work is finished.

**Deciding whether a use falls inside this section.** If the content is routine,
non-urgent, factually verified, attributed to the district as an institution
rather than to a named individual, and distributed through a district channel,
it is likely permitted. If any of those five is not true, treat the use as
prohibited or as requiring review and consult Section 6.

---

## 2. Prohibited uses

These are refused outright. They are not available with disclosure, with
approval, or with a compelling reason. A request to do any of these should be
declined by the employee receiving it and reported to the Communications
Director.

**2.1 Emergency, safety, and health notifications.** No synthetic voice or video
may be used in any communication concerning a lockdown, evacuation, shelter in
place, weather emergency, threat, injury, death, medical emergency, missing
student, or any other safety-critical event. These are recorded by a live human
being or delivered by a live human being, without exception, regardless of how
inconvenient that is in the moment.

This is the most important line in this policy. Emergency communication works
only if families act on it instantly. The moment a family has to wonder whether an
urgent call is real, the channel is worthless — and it will be worthless in the
one circumstance where we cannot afford it to be. We are not willing to spend any
part of that credibility, for any efficiency gain, in any non-emergency context
that might bleed into it.

**2.2 Replication of any student's voice or likeness.** Under no circumstances,
with no consent, from no one. Not with parent permission, not with student
assent, not for yearbooks, not for promotional material, not for instructional
use, not for a student's own project under staff supervision. Students are
minors; the district will not create or hold synthetic replicas of them, and will
not be the party that normalizes the practice for them.

**2.3 Replication of an employee's voice or likeness without documented
consent.** See Section 3. Absent a completed and current consent record, this is
prohibited, including for employees who have given verbal agreement, including
for senior administrators, including for the Superintendent.

**2.4 Replication of any individual outside the district.** Board members in
their personal capacity, parents, community members, public figures, vendors,
and anyone else not covered by a district consent record under Section 3.

**2.5 Synthetic content depicting events that did not occur.** No synthesized
audio or video representing a meeting, statement, conversation, incident, or
event that did not happen as depicted. This holds for illustrative, training, and
hypothetical purposes. If a training scenario requires a depicted incident, it is
performed by actors or animated, not synthesized to realism.

**2.6 Political, electoral, and advocacy communication.** No synthetic media in
any communication concerning a bond measure, levy, board election, ballot
question, or advocacy position, including material that is merely adjacent to
one. The risk of a district voice being implicated in an election dispute is not
worth any production efficiency.

**2.7 Individualized disciplinary, academic, or health communication.** No
synthetic voice in any message to a family concerning a specific student's
discipline, academic standing, attendance intervention, special education
process, or health. These conversations require a person who can be called back
and asked a question.

**2.8 Unattributed or ambiguously attributed synthetic speech.** No distribution
of synthetic audio that does not identify itself under Section 4. There is no
permitted use of undisclosed synthetic voice at Maplewood Unified.

## 3. Consent workflow

This section applies whenever a real person's voice, likeness, or identifiable
characteristics are replicated. Per Section 2.2, it never applies to students.

**3.1 Who may consent.** Current district employees aged 18 or over, for their
own voice or likeness, for use in district communications only.

**3.2 How consent is obtained.** In writing, on the district's Synthetic Voice
and Likeness Consent Form, signed by the employee and countersigned by the
Communications Director. Verbal consent is not valid under this policy. Consent
given in an email thread is not valid under this policy.

**3.3 What the consent form must specify.** The form is void unless it states all
of the following: the specific purposes for which the replica may be used; the
specific communication channels on which it may be distributed; the categories of
content it may not be used for, which must include at minimum everything in
Section 2; who holds the model files and where they are stored; the expiration
date; and the revocation process.

**3.4 Consent is narrow by default.** Consent for one purpose is not consent for
another. An employee who consented to a synthetic voice for translated enrollment
reminders has not consented to its use for a fundraising message. A new purpose
requires a new form.

**3.5 Duration.** Consent expires twelve months from signature. It does not
auto-renew. It terminates immediately and automatically when the employee leaves
the district, changes roles in a way that makes the original purpose
inapplicable, or revokes it.

**3.6 Revocation.** An employee may revoke at any time, in writing, to the
Communications Director, without giving a reason and without any approval
required. On revocation: the Technology Department deletes all voice model files
and training data within five business days and confirms deletion in writing to
the employee; all previously distributed material using the replica is withdrawn
from district channels within ten business days; and material that has already
reached families is not recalled but is not redistributed.

Employees must be told at the point of signature that revocation cannot retrieve
what has already been distributed. If that limitation is not acceptable to the
employee, they should not sign.

**3.7 No consequence for refusal.** An employee who declines to provide consent,
or who revokes it, faces no adverse employment consequence of any kind. Refusal
is not noted in any personnel record. Supervisors may not request consent from
their own direct reports; all requests are routed through the Communications
Office to remove the power differential from the conversation.

**3.8 Documentation.** The Communications Office maintains the consent register:
every current consent, its scope, its expiration, and every revocation. The
register is reviewed quarterly and expired entries are acted on, not merely
noted.

## 4. Disclosure standards

**4.1 In-signal disclosure is required.** Every piece of synthetic audio
distributed by this district must begin with a spoken statement, in the same
language as the content, identifying itself. The required form is:

> "This message uses a computer-generated voice. It was written and approved by
> Maplewood Unified School District."

For synthetic video, an equivalent spoken statement plus a persistent on-screen
label for the duration of the content.

**4.2 Why in-signal and not metadata or labels.** Because everything else comes
off. A filename is not part of a file. A platform label is not part of a file. A
caption in an email is not part of the attachment. Audio gets forwarded,
re-encoded by messaging apps, excerpted, renamed, and downloaded away from the
page that described it — not by bad actors but by families doing ordinary things
with their phones. A spoken line inside the waveform travels with the content
through all of that. It costs three seconds. It is not optional.

**4.3 Disclosure may not be removed from excerpts.** Any district staff member
clipping or excerpting synthetic content for another use must retain the spoken
disclosure or re-add it. An excerpt without disclosure may not be distributed.

**4.4 Supplementary labeling.** In addition to in-signal disclosure, synthetic
content is labeled in its filename, in the body of any email or post carrying it,
and in any platform-provided synthetic-media field. These are supplements. None
of them satisfies 4.1 on its own.

**4.5 Plain language.** Disclosure is written for families, not for lawyers.
"Computer-generated voice" is required phrasing. "AI-assisted," "synthetically
enhanced," and similar are prohibited as evasive.

## 5. Provenance requirements

**5.1 Content credentials where available.** Any tool used to produce synthetic
media under this policy must embed C2PA content credentials where the tool
supports it, and the Technology Department must verify embedding on a sample of
each production run rather than relying on vendor documentation.

**5.2 Provenance does not substitute for disclosure.** Verification testing on
commonly available synthesis tools has found that many embed no provenance
metadata at all, and that metadata which is embedded does not reliably survive
re-encoding by messaging platforms and social services. Content credentials are
therefore treated in this policy as useful for proving our own outputs are ours
after the fact — not as a mechanism families will ever see. Section 4 is the
disclosure mechanism. Section 5 is a record-keeping mechanism.

**5.3 Production log.** The Communications Office maintains, for every synthetic
item distributed: the date, the tool and version used, the full script as
synthesized, the employee who wrote it, the employee who approved it, the
consent record ID if a replica was used, the distribution channels, and a copy
of the final file as distributed. Retained three years.

**5.4 Master copies.** The district retains the distributed version of every
synthetic item so that a circulating clip can be compared against the authentic
original. This is the practical defense against a district communication being
altered and re-shared, and it is the main reason 5.3 exists.

## 6. Review and approval

**6.1 Standing approval.** Uses falling squarely within 1.1, 1.2, and 1.5 may be
produced by Communications Office staff without case-by-case approval, within
the terms of this policy.

**6.2 Communications Director approval.** All other permitted uses require
written approval from the Communications Director before production.

**6.3 Superintendent approval.** Any use involving an employee voice replica
under Section 3, and any use not clearly addressed by this policy, requires
Superintendent approval in addition to 6.2.

**6.4 The standard reviewers apply.** The reviewer asks, in order:

1. Is this use prohibited under Section 2? If yes, it is declined. No further
   analysis.
2. Could a human being record this instead, at reasonable cost? If yes, they
   should. Synthetic voice is for things a human recording cannot practically
   achieve — eleven languages, a deadline measured in hours, an accessibility
   need — not for saving a principal fifteen minutes.
3. If this clip were stripped of its disclosure and forwarded to a parent out of
   context, what would they believe? If the answer is alarming, the use is
   declined regardless of how well-disclosed the original was.
4. Does the content concern a specific child, a safety matter, or an election? If
   yes, see Section 2.

**6.5 Question three is the operative test.** Most proposals that fail this
policy fail at question three. Reviewers should apply it literally: imagine the
clip arriving on a parent's phone with no context, no label, and no filename.

**6.6 When in doubt, decline.** A reviewer who is uncertain declines. This
district has functioned without synthetic media; the cost of declining a
borderline use is that someone records something themselves.

## 7. Refusal

This district declines to produce synthetic media, including for otherwise
permitted uses, under any of the following conditions:

**7.1** The employee whose voice would be replicated has not signed a current
consent form, or has expressed any hesitation about it, regardless of whether a
valid form exists.

**7.2** The content concerns student safety, a specific student, an emergency, or
an election. See Section 2.

**7.3** The tool proposed cannot embed provenance metadata *and* the content is
intended for public distribution beyond district channels.

**7.4** The production timeline does not allow for the review in Section 6. A
deadline is not a reason to skip review. If there is no time to review it, there
is no time to synthesize it, and someone records it live.

**7.5** The request originates outside the district — from a vendor, a partner
organization, a media outlet, or a community group — and asks the district to
produce or lend a synthetic voice for their use.

**7.6** The Communications Director judges that the use would erode family trust
in district communications, even if it satisfies every other provision of this
policy. This is a discretionary refusal and it requires no further justification.

## 8. Incident response

An incident is any of the following: synthetic content produced by this district
circulating without its disclosure; synthetic content falsely attributed to this
district or to a district employee; a voice replica used outside its consented
scope; or synthetic content reaching an audience it was not intended for.

**8.1 Report immediately** to the Communications Director and the Technology
Director. Any employee who becomes aware of an incident reports it, including the
employee who caused it. There is no disciplinary consequence for self-reporting
an incident promptly and in good faith; the policy is written this way
deliberately, because an incident the district learns about in hour one is
survivable and one it learns about in week two generally is not.

**8.2 Within four hours:** confirm whether the content originated with the
district by comparing against the master copies held under 5.4. Determine the
scope of circulation.

**8.3 Within twenty-four hours:** issue a correction through every channel the
content reached, plus the district website. The correction is delivered in a
human voice or in writing, never synthetically. If an employee's voice was
replicated without consent or outside scope, notify that employee before any
public statement.

**8.4 If an employee's voice was used without consent,** the district supports
that employee including, at their request, with legal referral, and the voice
model is deleted immediately under 3.6 regardless of the state of any consent
record.

**8.5 Within thirty days:** written incident review covering what happened, which
control failed, and what changes to this policy follow. Delivered to the
Superintendent and the Board. Section 9 is updated if the incident revealed a
gap.

**8.6 Content falsely attributed to the district.** If synthetic content purports
to be from Maplewood Unified but was not produced by us, the district states so
publicly, specifically, and quickly. We can do this because of 5.3 and 5.4 —
we know exactly what we have produced. That capability is the practical reason
for the production log, and it is the main thing this policy gives us against an
external bad actor.

---

## 9. Compliance

This policy is a condition of employment for staff who produce district
communications and a contractual requirement for vendors. Violations are handled
through ordinary disciplinary processes, with the exception in 8.1 for prompt
self-reporting.

The Communications Director reviews the consent register and production log
quarterly and reports annually to the Board on volume of synthetic content
produced, consents active and revoked, incidents, and any provision of this
policy that has proven unworkable in practice.

Questions about whether a proposed use is permitted go to the Communications
Office before production, not after.
